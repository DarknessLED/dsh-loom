## What the loop could not do for itself

Four defects were unlocked by hand, because a loop that cannot spawn, cannot read
its ledger, or whose guard is blind cannot repair the thing that blinds it:

- `startActivation` takes the delegation request **flat** on 0.2.2-rc.3
  (`1e4d7dc`); the old nested `{request}` shape made every spawn die at
  `rootOf(undefined)`, and the host's one-line error hid a file name for eight rounds.
- `usageOfSession` could not read the `.zstd` ledger, and the ledger is a
  **sequence of frames** (`c250730`, `1fa5353`); a single-frame read returned 270
  bytes out of 161 KB. One round billed at $0.0676 instead of $0.0007 — a factor
  of 90.
- The pricing table did not exist (`cordis.patch.yml`). `priceSource` now reads
  `model:deepseek-flash`; per-relay rows are in place and all three relays are
  billed at DeepSeek's official rate, per instruction.
- `src/gate/gate.js` could promote an empty patch and count a sibling solve as a
  death; that was the first thing the specs above closed.

## The rule this leaves behind

A change to the loop is a task with a verifier, or it is not a change. Every row
above started as a red spec in `tests/self*/`, was bought by a round whose verifier
was that spec, and only then became a pin in `scripts/test-all.mjs` — so the loop
cannot un-earn it in a later round.

## The payload that could not be sent

A session hit `payload core text alone is ~18.6 MB … no upstream can accept it`
and kept hitting it: every retry re-sent the same body. The plugin is not allowed
to be the reason a request cannot go out, so three boundaries are now hard:

- `src/core/limits.js` — `PAYLOAD.promptSection` 4 KB, `PAYLOAD.toolResult` 48 KB,
  `PAYLOAD.field` 2 KB, `PAYLOAD.tail` 1.2 KB. `clipSection` caps the system-prompt
  status block and says how many characters it withheld; `clipDeep` caps a tool
  result and names the fields it cut. `rsi_status` in `detail:'full'` mode used to
  return the record untouched — it is clipped now, and reports `truncated:{bytes,fields}`.
- `failureDigest` — a guard failure used to keep the last 400 characters of the
  output, which is the summary line and never the failing suite. Two p6 promotions
  were rolled back invisibly for exactly that reason. The digest now keeps the
  `FAIL`/`AssertionError` lines first, then the head and tail, under 1.6 KB.
- Spec: `tests/self21/payload-budget.test.mjs` (10 tests) pins all of it, and is a
  standing pin — the caps cannot be raised back to "unbounded" by a later round.

What this does *not* fix: the transcript itself. That session holds ~100 MB of
assistant messages across ~10,000 steps, so the request is too large before the
plugin adds anything. A fresh session is the remedy for the history; the caps above
are the remedy for the plugin repeating it.

Two things found while doing it, both written down rather than smoothed over:

- `tests/self2/plateau.test.mjs` was red and had been outside `scripts/test-all.mjs`
  for as long as the scan has skipped `tests/self*`. The predicate now treats a
  missing round record as "no verdict" (flat) and the spec is pinned.
- The benchmark that finally showed a saving (`bench-opt` $0.025992 vs
  `bench-base` $0.059771, same score 1.0) needed three attempts to be honest:
  the first comparison was invalid because the plugin predated screen billing,
  the second because both campaigns shared one workspace and one arm's promoted
  patch fixed the other arm's base. The numbers above are from two fresh clones,
  one round each, run in sequence.
- It replicated on two more fresh pairs (`bench2`, `bench3`): all six rounds
  promoted at score 1.0, totals **$0.128140 vs $0.079358 (38.1% cheaper,
  62.1% fewer tokens)**, per-pair saving 56.5% / 25.5% / 14.3%. The variance is
  the point: the base arm often wins on its first attempt and then pays for
  further full attempts, while the opt arm stops after the survivor it bought
  first; in seed 3 the base arm burned $0.008588 on a second attempt that scored
  0. Still one seed repo, one round per campaign — a replicated demonstration,
  not a distribution.
